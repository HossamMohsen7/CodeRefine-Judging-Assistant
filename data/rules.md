OVERVIEW - DESCRIPTION

- IEEE Victoris 5.0 is an event organized by IEEE Mansoura Student Branch and powered by the National Telecom Regulatory Authority (NTRA) to foster innovation, collaboration, and excellence in technology.
- The event includes competitions, mentorships, and interactive sessions designed to enhance participants' technical expertise and problem-solving abilities.
- Participants will engage in hands-on challenges and real-world projects, applying their theoretical knowledge to gain practical experience.
- The focus on teamwork, leadership, and professional development aims to empower participants and inspire future technological leaders.

OVERVIEW - COMPETITION

- IEEE Victoris 5.0 includes various competitions, with one of its highlights being CodeRefine, a software engineering contest where teams "rescue" broken systems.
- Teams receive buggy, inefficient codebases and must debug, refactor, optimize, and deliver polished, high performance applications.
- Section 2 details the CodeRefine Competition.

CODEREFINE - DESCRIPTION

- CodeRefine is a unique software engineering competition where participants are challenged to rescue broken codebases. Instead of starting from scratch, teams receive malfunctioning, inefficient systems and must fix, refactor, and optimize them into clean, high-quality solutions, across two tracks: algorithmic problem solving and system design.
- This competition bridges two core pillars of real-world software engineering, algorithmic problem solving and system design, challenging participants to think both in code efficiency and architectural clarity.

CODEREFINE - OBJECTIVES

- Strengthen debugging, refactoring, and optimization skills.
- Gain hands-on experience with maintaining and improving existing codebases.
- Practice clean code principles and professional-grade development.
- Compete in a realistic, high-pressure environment that mirrors real tech jobs.
- Learn how to analyze, design, and deliver complete system improvements.
- Develop system design thinking: analyze, architect, and redesign broken systems at a high level.
- Learn to evaluate trade-offs in data modeling, API design, and high-level architecture.

CODEREFINE - TARGET AUDIENCE

- High school and university students with experience in one of the following programming languages: C++, Java, Python, JavaScript, or C#.

TEAMS & WORKSPACE - TEAM FORMATION

- Teams may consist of 1 to 3 members, and interdisciplinary collaboration is encouraged.
- A mandatory Team Leader will coordinate activities and serve as the primary contact.
- It is encouraged for team members to collaborate and support each other across roles, fostering an environment of knowledge sharing and collective problem-solving.

TIMELINE & PHASES - PHASE STRUCTURE

The competition phases are:
- Preparation Sessions: online, 2 hours each.
- Qualifier 1, Problem Solving: online, 3 hours.
- Qualifier 2, System Design: online, 3 hours.
- Finals: in person, 5 hours.

- Each solved problem in the problem-solving phase is scored instantly, and your live ranking updates in real time.
- The final hour is a blind hour: the leaderboard is frozen; final ranks are revealed at the award ceremony.

TIMELINE & PHASES - DURATION AND COMMUNICATION

- The duration of each phase will be communicated during orientation. Teams must adhere to the timeline and deadlines.
- Clear communication channels will be established for updates and instructions.
- By following the defined phase structure and timeline, teams can effectively manage their time, allocate resources, and make steady progress towards the successful completion of the competition.

EVALUATION CRITERIA - QUALIFIER 1 PROBLEM SOLVING

- Qualifier 1, Problem Solving: Correctness (passes test cases), performance (meets time/memory limits).

EVALUATION CRITERIA - QUALIFIER 2 SYSTEM DESIGN

Qualifier 2, System Design is scored on these criteria:
- Functional & Non-functional Requirements (10%)
- Data Model (20%)
- API Design (20%)
- High-level Architecture (25%)
- Deep Dives (25%)
- Back-of-the-envelope Estimation (bonus)

EVALUATION CRITERIA - FINALS

- Finals, Early Problems (1 - 9): Correctness and performance, automated.
- Finals, Problem 10: Functionality, Code Quality, Optimization, README Clarity, Creativity & Scalability.
- Finals, Problem 10 (System Design): Same criteria as Qualifier 2: Functional & Non-functional Requirements, Data Model, API Design, High-level Architecture, Deep Dives.

EVALUATION CRITERIA - OVERALL QUALIFIER SCORE

- Overall Qualifier Score: Problem Solving carries 40% of the total qualifier score, System Design carries 60%.

JUDGING SYSTEM - QUALIFIER 1 PROBLEM SOLVING

- Qualifier 1, Problem Solving: Automated testing, each test case = 1 point. Live scoreboard visible until the blind hour.

JUDGING SYSTEM - QUALIFIER 2 SYSTEM DESIGN

Qualifier 2, System Design is evaluated through a hybrid judging system:
- An AI model developed by IEEE automatically evaluates each submission against the criteria and generates a detailed report, including what was done well and a breakdown of every point deducted and why.
- A human judge then reviews exclusively the negative scores flagged by the AI to verify their accuracy and ensure fairness before scores are finalized.
- Final scores are released only after both qualifiers are completed.

JUDGING SYSTEM - COMBINED SCORE AND FINALS

- Combined Qualifier Score: Shown on the leaderboard only after both qualifiers are done. Problem Solving = 40%, System Design = 60%.
- Finals Problems 1 - 9: Automated testing, each test case = 1 point.
- Finals Problem 10, System Design: Same hybrid judging system as Qualifier 2: AI evaluation with full report, human judge confirms negative scores before finalization.
- Finals Score: Sum of automated scores (Problems 1 - 9) + finalized AI + human score (Problem 10). Evaluated separately and not included in the ongoing leaderboard.
- All judges' decisions are final and binding.
